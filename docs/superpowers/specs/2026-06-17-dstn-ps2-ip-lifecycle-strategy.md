## Offeror's IP strategy up to and following commercial launch

The Offeror's IP strategy is staged to match each commercialization phase, from initial domestic deployment through eventual international and enterprise scale.

**Domestic commercialization measures:** Canadian provisional patents (Months 1–3) are converted to full non-provisional filings before the 12-month priority deadline, timed to the Pathway to Commercialization window when DSTN moves from PSPC to other federal departments (SSC, ISED) and provincial procurement bodies. `did:stratos` remains on the W3C DID registry as the reference implementation, reinforcing Stratos as the canonical issuer for any GC department adopting the standard. The DSTN application layer is licensed to GC departments under SaaS subscription terms (per the Business Plan's revenue model) rather than sold or open-sourced, keeping copyright and trade secret protections intact while enabling deployment.

**International commercialization measures:** a Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) filing is made before the Canadian priority window closes, preserving the option for national-phase patent filings in the US, EU, and UK once Track B international targets (SAM.gov, PEPPOL, UK GDS) are confirmed as viable, avoiding upfront filing costs in jurisdictions without confirmed market entry. `did:stratos`'s W3C registration is itself an international-readiness measure, since the standard requires no separate national IP filing to be referenced by foreign procurement systems.

**Measures for detecting infringement:** the immutable Stratos Decentralized Database audit log and on-chain schema/credential-definition anchoring make any unauthorized clone of the DSTN credential format independently observable — a competing `did:stratos`-anchored credential definition would be visible on the public blockchain. The Offeror also monitors W3C DID registry submissions and Hyperledger Aries ecosystem announcements for derivative implementations referencing the patented protocols, and reviews public GC procurement vehicle listings for unauthorized use of the DSTN name or protected protocol descriptions.

**Measures for enforcing IP rights:** cease-and-desist correspondence is the first step for suspected copyright or trade secret misuse, followed by formal infringement action through Canadian courts (Federal Court for patent matters) where warranted. For the W3C standard itself, `did:stratos` is intentionally not exclusionary — enforcement there is reputational and commercial (being the reference implementation and original network), not legal, consistent with the strategic decision to keep that asset open rather than patented.
