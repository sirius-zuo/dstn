## Offeror's IP strategy to protect IP generated or utilized by the proposed innovation

The Offeror's IP strategy matches a small, ISC-stage company's resources: protect the highest-value novel processes now, defer costly international filings until commercialization is validated, and rely on copyright/trade secret rather than patenting for fast-moving application code.

**Foreground IP identification and protection:** Four new assets are created under this contract — the `did:stratos` DID method specification, the TEE-attested credential issuance protocol, the Proof-of-Traffic-anchored revocation registry method, and the passkey-gated TEE Holder Agent protocol — each tracked against the milestone plan as it is built. Protection options selected per asset: the three protocol-level inventions (TEE issuance, PoT revocation, passkey-gated holder) are filed as Canadian provisional patent applications in Months 1–3, establishing priority before any public demonstration. `did:stratos` is deliberately not patented; it is submitted to the W3C DID registry as an open standard, a strategic choice that creates permanent attribution and reference-implementation status rather than an exclusionary right. The DSTN application layer (Supplier Portal, Verification API, Verifier Widget) is protected as proprietary copyright and trade secret, appropriate for fast-iterating software where patent prosecution timelines would lag the build schedule.

**Background IP review and freedom to operate:** Stratos Dcloud infrastructure and the Proof-of-Traffic consensus mechanism are existing Stratos / DEC Foundation assets (trade secret and patent-candidate respectively), with no third-party encumbrance. Third-party components were reviewed for freedom to operate: Hyperledger Aries/AnonCreds (Apache 2.0 — no restriction on commercial or government use), the W3C VC Data Model (royalty-free standard), and TEE hardware interfaces (Intel SGX/AMD SEV — standard hardware APIs, no software IP encumbrance). No licensing or freedom-to-operate barriers were identified.

**Relevant jurisdictions:** Canada is the primary and immediate jurisdiction, matching the ISC contract's domestic scope and the company's current stage. `did:stratos`, as a W3C standard, is inherently international by nature of the registry itself. Filings in the United States, European Union, and United Kingdom are deferred until Phase 2/3 commercialization (Track B) is underway, avoiding premature international filing costs disproportionate to current company size.

**Jurisdiction-specific requirements:** Canadian provisional patents are filed with the Canadian Intellectual Property Office (CIPO), establishing a 12-month priority window. Before that window closes, a Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) filing is planned if Phase 2 commercialization confirms international market entry, preserving the option to pursue national-phase filings in the US, EU, and UK without re-establishing priority dates.
