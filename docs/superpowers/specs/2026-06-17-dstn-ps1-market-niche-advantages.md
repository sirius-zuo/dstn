## How the proposed innovation creates competitive advantages in existing market niches or market spaces

DSTN creates durable competitive advantage across three market niches, each currently served by a different incumbent or substitute:

1. **Government-to-business credentialing** (currently served by manual PSPC processes and BC Government's OrgBook BC / Verifiable Organizations Network on Hyperledger Indy). DSTN's advantage compounds with adoption rather than staying static: every verification call against a credential adds to an on-chain, network-observed Proof-of-Traffic trust signal that a new entrant starting from zero cannot replicate — the network's value grows the longer it operates, unlike a manually-run registry where each lookup is a one-off cost with no accumulating asset. Submitting `did:stratos` to the W3C DID registry also raises switching costs once referenced in procurement policy: replacing the DID method becomes a policy change, not a vendor swap, which is a structural lock-in advantage no permissioned-ledger competitor (Indy-based or otherwise) can claim from a generic ledger choice.

2. **Enterprise VC infrastructure** (served by Microsoft Entra Verified ID, Dock.io, Trinsic, Spruce ID, and Canadian integrator Northern Block). These platforms compete on developer features and generic issuance tooling; DSTN competes on holder UX — a wallet-free, passkey-anchored holder agent removes the install/seed-phrase barrier that depresses adoption among non-technical small suppliers. In a market segment (government SME suppliers) where holder adoption, not issuer tooling, is the bottleneck, this UX advantage is structural: competitors would need to rebuild their holder architecture around TEE-backed passkey identity to match it, not simply add a feature.

3. **Static trust-seal/certification badges** (TrustPilot, BBB accreditation, SSL trust seals, and basic centralized registries like SAM.gov or SAP Ariba). These compete on brand recognition and simple lookup convenience; DSTN's badge is a live, cryptographically verifiable claim rather than a static image or simple database flag, so it cannot go stale, be reused after revocation, or be embedded fraudulently without failing verification — a credibility advantage that pure UX-pattern competitors cannot match without rebuilding on a credential protocol.

Underlying all three: Stratos's existing TRL-9 production decentralized infrastructure gives DSTN a structural cost and resilience advantage over any competitor building on centralized cloud (AWS/Azure), since Stratos avoids both the single-region failure risk and the centralized cloud cost structure that constrains commercial VC vendors' margins at scale.
